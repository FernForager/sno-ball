# Plan v2: Washington edition

Supersedes `docs/PLAN-v1.md` wherever the two differ. v1's stack (Vite + TypeScript, MapLibre GL JS, OHM tiles with `maplibre-gl-dates`, OpenFreeMap basemap, Vitest + Playwright, GitHub Actions to Pages), its story-first UX, its gear/stop time model, its caching rules and its deploy workflow all stay. What changes is scope (Washington only, Seattle deepest), the data strategy (pre-bake at build time, fetch little at runtime), and the time stops (Washington's own story).

---

# PART 1 — In plain English

**What changes, and why it is better.** The first plan tried to tell the story of any address on Earth by asking a dozen live services every time someone typed. Plan v2 draws the line at Washington's borders. The idea is unchanged: type an address, read the history of that exact spot from the house outward, and turn the time dial. But because the region is bounded, most of the data can be cooked once and stored inside the site. A script (run by GitHub itself, once a month) downloads public datasets, trims them to Washington, and commits small files. A visitor's phone then loads a handful of our own files instead of waiting on strangers' servers. Only four things are fetched live: the address lookup, the parcel record, the rock at the spot, and photos.

Washington also happens to be a gift. King County publishes the year every house was built (about 525,000 homes, plus the name of the "addition" the lot was platted in). Seattle publishes its 94 neighborhoods, the year each piece of the city was annexed, 517 landmarks, and which ground is fill or regraded hillside. The state geological survey maps the rock under every parcel, and that rock tells a dramatic story: a mile of ice over Seattle 17,000 years ago, floods that put the Tri-Cities under 800 feet of water, lava that buried a third of the state, a magnitude-9 earthquake in 1700 whose tsunami was recorded in Japan. HistoryLink.org has a dated essay for every county, most towns and most Seattle neighborhoods. The USGS has real maps of the state from 1894 onward, so "the map as it was in 1897" is a real map, not a guess.

**A Seattle address.** Your house: built 1926, Lot 7 Block 12 of Denny's Second Addition, on glacial till. Your lot joined Seattle on May 29, 1907 with Ballard. Your neighborhood by its colloquial and official names, its HistoryLink history, a 1930s photo. The city: population every decade since 1860, the 1889 fire, the regrades, the 1916 lowering of Lake Washington. The 1897 map shows the tideflats where the stadiums now stand. Turn the dial to 15,000 BCE: "your spot was under about 3,000 feet of ice."

**A small town in eastern Washington.** Say Ritzville. The house ring is thinner: parcel number, land use, and a link to the county assessor's record. But the town's founding and incorporation year, its population every decade since 1890, the county's creation (Adams, 1883, carved from Whitman), the treaty that ceded the land (Walla Walla council, June 1855), the 1909 map, the 1980 ashfall, and the rock: Columbia River Basalt under Missoula-flood gravel, with a 49-million-year-old fossil lake two hours north.

**What stays thin.** Year built outside King County (a link until each county's file is added); street-name origins outside Seattle; ordinary blocks; photos outside the big cities. Thin rings say so plainly and point outward. Before 1775 the human story is Indigenous history, told with the nations' own websites and without invented boundaries.

**What it costs.** $0. No server, no accounts, no API keys, no credit card. Same as before.

**What you must do.** One thing: Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source → "GitHub Actions". (Later, click "Merge" when asked.)

**Build order.**
- Milestone 1 (one working session; mostly built already): type any Washington address and see your rings with names, your county's founding, the rock under you, the ice-age fact, today's map, and in Seattle the year built. Live at the Pages link.
- Milestone 2: the time dial with 27 named Washington stops, the two timelines, "not yet" for towns, and the "map as it was" layer (1897, 1909, 1949...).
- Milestone 3: year built for every King County house, plat names, the statewide parcel lookup, Seattle's neighborhoods and annexations baked in, populations by decade, county borders by date.
- Milestone 4: photos, HistoryLink links, place-name origins, old street names, the geology and ice-age overlays, the About page. Public launch.
- Milestone 5: other counties' year built, streetcar lines, plat years, polish.

**King County year-built shards.** Input: five zips (~60 MB), stream-parsed as latin1 with trimmed values, `Major`/`Minor` kept as 6- and 4-character strings (never coerced to numbers), CSV name matched by `/^EXTR_(ResBldg|CommBldg2?|Parcel|AptComplex|CondoComplex)\.csv$/i`; the script fails loudly if any expected header column is missing. Output `public/data/kc/yrbuilt/<first 3 digits of Major>.json`:

```json
{"v":1,"asOf":"2026-10-01","majors":{"197520":{"plat":"DENNY'S A A 2ND ADD","complex":[1962,1962,1,24],
 "lots":{"0495":[1906,1962,0,10,2200,8,3,2,"Restaurant/Lounge","7","12"]}}}}
```

Lot array = `[yrBuiltOldest, yrBuiltNewest, yrRenovated, stories×10, sqftLiving, grade, condition, nBldgs, presentUse, platLot, platBlock]` from ResBldg (min/max `YrBuilt` per PIN, drop `YrBuilt=0`, flag 1900 as `placeholder` in the UI copy), CommBldg (`YrBuilt`, `EffYr`), Parcel (`PlatName,PlatLot,PlatBlock,PresentUse`). `complex` = `[yrBuilt, yrBuilt, nBldgs, nUnits]` from Apartment/Condo Complex rows keyed by `Major`; condo units (`Minor != 0000` under a complex `Major`) and unplatted land (`Minor 9xxx`, `Major` = section/township/range) resolve through `majors[major]` when `lots[minor]` is absent. Any shard over 150 KB is split to a 4-digit prefix (the index of split prefixes lives in `kc/yrbuilt/index.json`); only changed shards are rewritten so monthly diffs stay small. Runtime: KCGIS point query → zero-pad `PIN` to 10 → fetch one shard → `lots[minor]` else `majors[major]` → render. In Seattle, `PARCEL_GEO.YR_BUILT_MAX` is shown alongside when it disagrees ("assessor records 1906; newest structure 1962"). The same shard gives the block ring for free: all `lots` of a `Major` are one plat, so "most houses in Denny's Second Addition were built in the 1900s" is a local aggregation. Terms: show "Data provided by permission of King County" and "King County Assessor"; no owner names exist in these files; site stays non-commercial.

**Per-county fallback.** Outside King County the house ring shows parcel number, situs address, DOR land-use description (`LANDUSE_CD` 11 = single family…), "there is a structure here" when `VALUE_BLDG > 0`, the county's `FILE_DATE`, and "See the county assessor's record (year built, buildings)" built from `DATA_LINK`. When a `counties/<fips>/yrbuilt` shard set exists for that county, `ORIG_PARCEL_ID` (hyphens stripped) is the key. ## 6. Event data model

Two bundled files, one shape (snake_case in the data files; `loadEvents()` maps to the existing `HistoryEvent` interface):

```ts
interface EventRecord {
  id: string;                 // slug, stable across rebuilds
  title: string;
  when: string;               // human readable
  start_years_ago: number;    // years before 2000 CE (negative = after 2000; -50000000 = 50 Ma in the future)
  end_years_ago: number;      // same convention; equals start for a moment; 0 = continuing
  region: string[];           // scope tokens: "statewide" | a WaRegion name | "<Name> County" | a city name
  lat: number | null;         // anchor point for {distance}; null when there is no single place
  lon: number | null;
  summary: string;            // our own words (facts); never Wikipedia/HistoryLink prose
  at_your_spot: string | null;// template; dropped if any placeholder cannot be resolved
  spot_rule?: 'flood-deposits' | 'vashon-ice' | 'osceola-lahar' | 'electron-lahar' | 'crbg' | 'seattle-fault-near' | 'annexed-in-range' | 'incorporated-in-range';
  sources: { title: string; url: string }[];
  confidence: 'high' | 'medium' | 'low';
}
```

**Region filter.** An event shows for a spot when `region` contains `"statewide"`, the spot's `WaRegion`, its county ring name, or its city ring name. Deep-time events render in the region/state/plate rings; human events in the city/county/region/state rings; each ring shows only events whose window overlaps the selected stop's window (stop ± half the gear step), newest first, with v1's QUIET state and ◀ ▶ neighbour links from the same filtered list.

**Placeholders** resolved by `renderTemplate()`: `{place}` (house ring name or "your spot"), `{distance}` (great-circle from the spot to `lat,lon`, formatted "3 miles" / "140 miles" / "a few hundred feet"), `{county}`, `{city}`, `{inc_year}` (city incorporation year), `{annex_year}` (Seattle annexation year), `{ice_thickness}` (interpolated from Thorson 1980: 5,000 ft Bellingham, 3,000 ft Seattle, 2,000 ft Tacoma, 1,000 ft Olympia, 660 ft at Tenino; "about"), `{basalt_unit}` and `{basalt_thickness}` (from the DNR unit full name; thickness only when the description states one), `{flood_depth}` (1,200 ft minus elevation from Open-Meteo elevation, Phase 5; until then the rule renders "under hundreds of feet of water"), `{nearest_volcano}` (nearest of Baker, Glacier Peak, Rainier, St. Helens, Adams; constants). Human-timeline lines use only `{distance}`, `{inc_year}`, `{annex_year}`.

**Spot rules** gate `at_your_spot` on what the DNR unit says: `flood-deposits` = symbol starts with `Qf`; `vashon-ice` = symbol starts with `Qg` or the spot is north of the Tenino limit line (bundled); `crbg` = unit name contains "Columbia River Basalt" or symbol matches `/^M?v[bgw]/`; `osceola-lahar` / `electron-lahar` = unit description contains "Osceola" / "Electron"; `seattle-fault-near` = within 25 km of 47.58,−122.48; `annexed-in-range` / `incorporated-in-range` = the ring's year falls inside the event window. When the rule fails, the summary still shows (region match) but the second-person line does not.

**Dedupe.** Geologic events (Cascadia 1700, the 1949/1965/2001 quakes, St. Helens 1980) live only in `deep-time.json`; the human file does not repeat them. Two more pre-baked lists feed the city ring: `events/seattle-timeline.json` and `events/spokane-timeline.json`, `[{year, label, url}]` where `label` is our own ≤ 12-word paraphrase (Wikipedia prose is CC BY-SA and is never copied). Every rendered event shows its source names and links; HistoryLink appears as "Read the HistoryLink essay" links only.
